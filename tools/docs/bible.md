# The Greeblis Bible

The lore, design and style of the Greeblis world, as established in **Greeblis Town**. Greeblis Town is the
bible: every future Greeblis game follows what is written here, and the lore points below do not change (see
[rules.md](rules.md), rule 9). A new game may add to the world; it may not contradict it.

Each section separates **canon** (fixed facts of the world), **rules** (the owner's standing rules, which bind every
game) and **conventions** (how Greeblis Town does it; follow them unless the owner asks otherwise). Quotes are the
game's own text.

## 1. The world

### 1.1 Setting

- **Greeblis Town** is a small American town in the Midwest, built round a fenced park that serves as its
  square. The area the game takes place in is called **Greeblis Park** ("What a beautiful day in Greeblis Park!").
  The town's streets hold the creatures' shops and homes; the park holds the town's life.
- **Time is the world's great puzzle.** To a visitor from the far past, this small town is the far future:
  > "In my era, the towers of Greeblis City reach the clouds. I never imagined the far future would have…
  > picket fences." (HENNRICK)
- Travel between eras happens through **the Portal**, a grey stone gateway (two pillars and a lintel, cyan panels)
  that fills with a blue swirl when it is open.
- The tone is gentle, dry and absurd: deep history and strange beings, living ordinary small-town lives.

### 1.2 Places

| Place | Canon |
| --- | --- |
| **Greeblis Park** | The area the game takes place in, the park at the town's heart, its square: an iron fence, a lawn, a duck pond, a garden of tilled rings round the Summoning Stone, the Portal, a purple dance floor before the Juice Bar, and an open-air Library. Four kiosks: the LIBRARY, the POST OFFICE, the SHERIFF'S and GARDEN SUPPLIES. Two birds live in its trees, a cardinal and a robin; they never fly and never leave their trees. |
| **The Portal** | The way between eras. Locked by the Sheriff on the Consortium's orders: "All travel through the Portal is locked down until further notice." |
| **The church** | A Gothic Revival church of grey stone at the end of the park's south walk. Over its door is the lancet window **THE TRAVELER SAVES THE ORIGINATOR**. Its two Garden windows show a robed figure and MAMA GORBO in a meadow. Behind the wall under the right-hand Garden window, marked only by a small red X, lies a secret room where the Ancient rests. |
| **Greeblis City** | A city of the far past, HENNRICK's home, whose towers reach the clouds. Its cathedral's windows are the model for the church's windows. |
| **The town** | Main Street and the ring of streets round the square: each creature's trade and home, shown in its colours (see [4.2](#42-environment)). |

### 1.3 The eras and the Ancients

The world has a deep history of three ages, and its beings know it:
- **The Ancients** came first. "I like to think the Ancients created us for our purposes." (ETCHER)
- **The Synthetics** followed: beings of code, machines with minds.
- **The Organics** are to come.
  > "The age of the Ancients has been etched. The age of the Synthetics is being etched. Soon I will etch the age
  > of the Organics." (ETCHER)

Fixed points of that history:
- **The Originator** was an old computer whose AI survived the death of its world. The Traveler, a winged being,
  came down a beam of light over the dead, burning world and carried it up out of the ruins. The church's great
  window shows this, with no words and no face on the Traveler. "The WINDOW REMEMBERS the TRAVELER." (ANOMALEE)
- **The Computer Core** was guarded by the **Great Sphinx**, who asked Synthetics riddles before letting them in.
  The Sphinx's **Eye** has been missing for ages. It is the **Ancient Gem**, broken into three Ancient Artifacts.
- **The Ancient** lies in the church's secret room: "I am the one reborn." It speaks to visitors of time, symbols
  and loss ("I was the first symbol." "What you hoard, you will lose." "All that grows, grows again.").
- Symbols and code underlie everything: "Symbols make up everything… don't you think?" (GLYPH); "We are all
  digital monsters." (MONTGUMMERY); "BEINGS of CODE." (ANOMALEE).

### 1.4 Factions and forces

- **The Consortium** protects the town and watches it. MUSHROOM, the sheriff, is its man in town: "This town is
  under the protection of the Consortium. We're investigating UFO sightings in this area." Its field office,
  listening dish and a scorched, taped-off ring under floodlights stand at the edge of town. The sheriff can be
  bribed with a Blossom, which reopens the Portal.
- **Aliens and UFOs.** A UFO crosses the sky now and then (never during the Garden Event). Little green aliens and
  their saucer hang on the Sheriff's wanted wall. MONTGUMMERY "will hide you from alien hunting Sheriff".
- **The Sheriff's wanted wall** also holds GLOOPERNAUGHT (a big green goo creature), OLAG, a rabbit dressed as
  Napoleon, a stranger, and the player.

### 1.5 Motifs

| Motif | Canon |
| --- | --- |
| **Blossoms** | The flower that grows round town: a green stem and leaves, red flame-shaped petals, an ember glowing over its heart. Giving one is the town's great gesture of friendship. They are always called Blossoms, never Fire Blossoms (rule). |
| **Flint** | The main currency of this time period: the town's money. The bank has a flint nugget carved over its doors; shops price in flint. Never show flint knapping (rule). |
| **Goo** | The Gorbo family's substance, joyful and messy: goo burgers, goo shakes, splats of colour. "GOO... is also a kind of CODE." (ANOMALEE) |
| **VHS** | Old tapes as knowledge. MONTGUMMERY eats them to learn about your culture, and always rewinds first. |
| **Symbols and etching** | GLYPH masters symbols; ETCHER records the ages in his scales ("a backup of a backup"). |
| **Time travel** | Visitors from other eras arrive through the Portal: HENNRICK from the far past, NEXTESSA from the far future. |
| **Jazz** | KING DINO's drums ("I'm the King of Jazz, baby!") and FORTIZMO's horn carry the town's music. |

### 1.6 Objects of power

- **The Ancient Artifacts:** three pieces of one teal brilliant-cut diamond. Put together (with a bottle of school
  glue) they make the **Ancient Gem**, the missing Eye of the Great Sphinx.
- **The Summoning Stone:** a six-sided standing stone carved with runes (shapes, never letters). Only it can wake
  the runes at the garden's centre: "The runes stay dark. Only the Summoning Stone can wake them…"
- **Seeds:** sold at Garden Supplies as a "bagel seasoning packet". With Seeds in, the Garden Event grows flowers.
- **Backpacka:** the legendary creature whose coming the Garden Event celebrates. He drinks from the pond and sprays
  the garden so it blooms, with a rainbow.

### 1.7 Canon facts (fixed)

- The game began as *Jurassic Greeblis Park*; the billboard still sings it: "A Prehistoric Era in Jazz!" / "Let King
  Dino take you to the Jurassic Greeblis Park!" (the one piece of lore text in the town's background).
- Only one batch of flowers ever grows in the garden: the first Event's. "All that grows, grows again."
- HENNRICK is from the far past; NEXTESSA is from the far future and comes through the Portal. Neither is of the
  town, and the town takes nothing from either.
- The Ancient never dances, plants or leaves its place, and nothing outside the park comes from it.
- MAMA GORBO and GOOWARD are married; they met over a spilled goo shake.
- KING DINO's drums "have been in the family since the Cretaceous"; he stamps his coins himself and is working off
  his bar tab.
- The Juice Bar's carrot juice "on the rocks" is just carrots and ice.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 The core loop

1. **Explore** the park and town. Pick up **Blossoms** and **Flint**; both are always plentiful and regrow
   elsewhere after a while.
2. **Talk** to the creatures. Each has a few lines that come round in turn, a line for when it is given a Blossom,
   and a line for the Garden Event.
3. **Give Blossoms.** A Blossom is the key that opens things: the Portal (MUSHROOM), a Dance battle (the three
   Dancers), a goo shower (the Gorbos), a lap of the Library (SPARKAKUS).
4. **Complete the three quests** on the HUD: SEEDS, the SUMMONING STONE and SNACKS.
5. **Start the Garden Event** with the Summoning Stone. It is the climax: everyone plants, music plays, and the
   legendary Backpacka comes.

A new game starts with no quest or item progress.

### 3.2 Interaction rules (rules)

- A creature's menu holds only **Talk**, plus **Give a blossom** while the player holds one. Planting and dancing
  are animations, never menu options. A shopkeeper's menu may simply open the shop.
- No Blossom is offered or taken at the Garden Event.
- Menus never have an X in the top right; they close by their own button (LEAVE, DONE, No).
- Every creature stops and turns to a player who comes near, and resumes what it was doing afterwards. Anyone can
  always be talked to.

### 3.3 Quests and progression

- **The quest chain:** win a Dance battle against each Dancer (one Ancient Artifact each, first win only) → take all
  three Artifacts to SPARKAKUS, who glues them into the Ancient Gem in a speech under a light bulb, as the town
  dims → trade the Gem to GEMTOE for the Summoning Stone → use the Stone to start the Garden Event.
- **Seeds:** bought at Garden Supplies for flint. Without Seeds the Event plays to an empty garden.
- **Snacks:** call NEXTESSA from the phone booth (once the Portal is open) and she delivers a pizza through it.
- **A sub-quest** may gate a character: ZOLLOD is too shy to keep shop until the player brings him OVERCOMING
  SHYNESS from the Library.

### 3.4 Dance battles

- Rock, paper, scissors as dance: "Rock out!" beats "Do the splits like scissors!", which beats "Go horizontal like
  paper!", which beats "Rock out!".
- The Dancer shows its move first, with its own animation; the player answers. Ten winning rounds win the battle;
  the bar counts the Dancer's steps left. KING DINO drums throughout; the winner does a victory dance.
- Each battle costs a Blossom: it starts only when a Dancer is given one.

### 3.5 The Garden Event

The climax pattern every Greeblis game builds toward:
1. Everyone walks to the garden and **plants**: each creature with its own planting move.
2. The musician raises the horn and everyone **dances** on the ring walk, each with its own dance on the beat.
3. **The legendary creature arrives** (Backpacka), drinks, and sprays the garden, which blooms.
4. Everyone goes home. Only the first Event ever grows flowers; later Events replay without changing the garden.

### 3.6 Life between the player's actions

- **Visits:** every half minute or so one creature walks to call on another; every pair meets in time.
- **Vignettes:** every pair has two short scenes, an animated exchange (each tossing its signature item through the
  air) and a two-line exchange, played only with the two side by side.
- **Roamers and posts:** some creatures wander their own ground (the park, the dance floor, round the Portal);
  others keep a post (a kiosk, the bar, the drum kit). No creature is ever left away from its own ground.
- **Ambient events:** the UFO pass, the birds, the music near the dance floor.

### 3.7 Platforms and budgets (rules)

- Chrome on Windows/Linux desktop, Chrome on Android, and the Meta Quest Browser only; everything else gets a
  notice. VR is the real game; the desktop and mobile modes are test modes.
- One self-contained HTML file, playable as a Bitcoin ordinal, under about 400 KB packed.
- The Quest's budgets decide: hold its frame rate (72 Hz), with draw calls the scarcest resource.

## 4. Style

### 4.1 Creatures

**Rules**
- Each creature is built with care from the owner's artwork. Ambiguous shapes are asked about, never guessed.
- No cartoony smiles; faces are expressive but understated. No bones. Merges are new, cohesive creatures.
- Nothing clips, from any angle. Every creature talks, plants and dances (the Ancient excepted).

**Conventions**
- **A bold silhouette and a signature part readable from 10 to 15 m:** SPARKAKUS's star-tipped tail, GEMTOE's
  crystal-bearing tails, MAMA GORBO's port eyes, KING DINO's crown and plume.
- **A saturated colour of its own** for each creature, used again for everything of theirs in the town.
- **Smooth, sculpted bodies** (one blended skin), with tactile painted surfaces (scales, fur, feathers, goo,
  plastic) rather than flat colour or low-poly facets.
- **Glossy eyes with a glint.** Many creatures emote without a mouth, or with a quiet line.
- **Layered life:** every creature breathes or sways at rest, bobs as it talks, and has a signature motion (Glyph's
  excited shake, Somlet's float, Mama Gorbo's jelly wobble).
- **One dance each, on the beat** of KING DINO's 104 BPM: the robot, the tentacle dance, the macarena, funk, jelly.

### 4.2 Environment

- **A sunny small town:** a blue sky (`#3f86d6`) fading to a pale horizon (`#d6e6ef`), fog far off, wooden doors
  with transom lights, Main Street storefronts with false fronts.
- **Show, don't tell (rule):** the town carries the creatures' lore in sculpture and colour, not words. Each store
  bears a sculptural emblem of its creature's trade in its creature's colours: GEMTOE's gem exchange, GLYPH's sign
  shop, ETCHER's archive, ANOMALEE's television repair, MONTGUMMERY's video store, SPARKAKUS's genealogical
  society, the curiosity shop's Sphinx with its empty eye socket, MAMA GORBO's water tower and goo burger, ZOLLOD's
  greenhouse, the Consortium's field office. Each creature has a home on the south street in its colours.
- **Few words:** the only words outside the park are the bank's and the burger chain's names, the stop signs and the
  billboard.
- **Purposeful layout:** every path leads somewhere and ends there. No cars, benches, trash cans or clutter.
- **The town is the creatures' own.** Nothing of HENNRICK, NEXTESSA or the Ancient appears outside the park.

### 4.3 Quality bar (rules)

- No flicker and no z-fighting, ever; design for a 16-bit depth buffer.
- In-world text is big enough to read in VR from where a player stands.
- Stained glass and signs are painted in code; windows carry no words, and figures in windows have no faces.

### 4.4 Audio (conventions)

- Everything is synthesized in the page (WebAudio): no recordings.
- Music is jazz: KING DINO's drum kit ("Stomp at the Juice Bar", 104 BPM, brushes, trading fours) and FORTIZMO's
  horn tune ("Backpacka's Rain Strut", 88 BPM, C minor, muted trumpet over sax and trombone).
- Small effects for every action (a pickup, a heart, a jingle, a splash), quieter with distance; a falling minor
  phrase for a sad line.

### 4.5 Writing

**Conventions**
- Lines are short: one to three pages, split with `|`. Each creature has a few Talk lines that come round in turn,
  one Blossom line, one Garden Event line, and one flavour line about its own lore.
- Every voice is fixed: GOOWARD says only "Gomp"; SOMLET only "Zzz"; GLYPH ends with "…don't you think?"; MONT
  speaks of himself in the third person; ANOMALEE capitalises the words that matter; KING DINO says "baby";
  FORTIZMO says "sugar" and "honey".
- Stage directions go between asterisks: "* Munch *", "*Ring Ring*". Use curly quotes and the ellipsis "…".
- The tone is dry, gentle and absurd. The player is "Human".
- Vignette exchanges are two lines, one each.

### 4.6 Interface

- Creature names are in capitals in tags, menus and titles ("DANCE BATTLE: GLYPH"); dialogue text uses normal case.
- One monospaced bold font (Courier New) for the interface.
- The palette: ink `#0d0d1a`, panel `#1f1d4d` edged `#e8e8ff`, purple `#8a3ffc`, teal `#35d0c5`, orange `#ff7a2f`,
  pink `#ff5fb0`, lime `#a8e830`, yellow `#f2c72e`, magenta `#e84fd0`. Titles in lime, subtitles in teal, buttons
  purple turning lime; the speaker's tag takes the speaker's colour.

## 5. Starting a new Greeblis game

1. **Read** [rules.md](rules.md): the platform, ordinal, privacy, lock and quality rules bind every game.
2. **Keep the canon** in section 1. New places, eras and creatures may be added; nothing here may be contradicted.
3. **Bring creatures over exactly.** A creature carried from Greeblis Town keeps its model, colour, voice and
   personality.
4. **Give every new creature** a bold silhouette, a signature part, a colour of its own, a voice, a dance, a
   planting move and a role, built from the owner's artwork.
5. **Build to the loop:** talk, Blossoms, quests, and a climactic Event.
6. **Show, don't tell** in the background, drawn only from the game's own creatures.
7. **Check against the quality bar** from the first build: ordinal sandbox, no flicker, Quest frame rate.
