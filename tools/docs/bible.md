# The Greeblis Bible

The lore, design and style of the Greeblis world, as established in **Greeblis Town**. Greeblis Town is the
bible: every future Greeblis game follows what is written here, and the lore points below do not change (see
[rules.md](rules.md), rule 9). A new game may add to the world; it may not contradict it.

Each section separates **canon** (fixed facts of the world), **rules** (the owner's standing rules, which bind every
game) and **conventions** (how Greeblis Town does it; follow them unless the owner asks otherwise). Quotes are the
game's own text, exactly as written.

## 1. The world

Quotes in this section and the next are the game's own text, character for character; where a line runs over
several pages of the dialogue box, the pages are joined with a space.

### 1.1 Setting

**Canon**
- **Greeblis Town** is a small American town in the Midwest, built round a fenced park that serves as its square. The
  area the game takes place in is called **Greeblis Park**:
  > "What a beautiful day in Greeblis Park!" (MAMA GORBO)
- The park is the town's life: the creatures keep their posts, wander and gather there. The streets round it hold
  their shops, trades and homes; beyond them, open prairie runs to the horizon and the four streets run on as county
  roads with gravel shoulders.
- **Time is the world's great puzzle.** Greeblis Town sits in a long history of ages (1.8). To a visitor from the far
  past, this small town is the far future:
  > "In my era, the towers of Greeblis City reach the clouds. I never imagined the far future would have… picket
  > fences." (HENNRICK)
- Travel between eras happens through **the Portal** (1.3), which the Sheriff keeps locked on the Consortium's orders.
- **Flint** is the main currency of this time period: the town's money.
- The town's beings are **beings of code**: "BEINGS of CODE." (ANOMALEE); "We are all digital monsters."
  (MONTGUMMERY). They live ordinary small-town lives (a librarian, a bartender, a mail carrier, a sheriff, a
  burger-joint couple, a garden-shop keeper) on top of deep, strange history.
- **The tone** is gentle, dry and absurd: ancient relics fixed with school glue, a sheriff who takes bribes in
  flowers, a monster who rewinds a tape before eating it.

**Canon**
- Blossoms and Flint grow round the park and regrow elsewhere after a while. They never grow inside the garden's
  stone ring, on the dance floor, under a creature, or where HENNRICK strolls.

  over the town, then zips off over the far horizon. Its sunlit top is plain chrome with panel seams; its shaded
  underside glows faintly. It never comes during the Garden Event.

### 1.3 The Portal and the Summoning Stone

- **The Portal** is a grey stone gateway (two pillars and a lintel, with cyan panels) that fills with a blue swirl
  when it is open; its rings glow pale cyan under a blue light, to a rising chord.
  - It is locked: "All travel through the Portal is locked down until further notice." (MUSHROOM)
  - A Blossom bribes the Sheriff into opening it: "A bribe? …Portal is now open to your use." Until then, the phone
    cannot reach the far future: "*connection to the Portal seems blocked*".
  - HENNRICK, stranded on this side, strolls from spot to spot round it and stops to look at it. NEXTESSA comes
    through it with the pizza, and its lane is kept clear for her.
- **The Summoning Stone** (the place) is a six-sided standing stone on a round plinth at the garden's centre, with rows
  of rune strokes carved on three faces: shapes, never letters. The runes stay dark until the player uses the stone
  while holding the Summoning Stone (the item, 1.13). Then they light up and breathe softly ever after, and blaze while
  the Garden Event runs.
  > "The runes stay dark. Only the Summoning Stone can wake them and start the gardening event."
- **The Event flowers.** At the first Garden Event, one flower comes up for each creature that plants, of one of five
  kinds, drawn larger than life: creeping thistle (pink-mauve puffs, some gone to seed fluff, over spiny leaves),
  scentless mayweed (white-rayed daisies with domed yellow centres), chicory (sky-blue toothed flowers on stiff stems),
  common milkweed (a round umbel of dusky mauve flowers) and zebra mallow (pale pink flowers streaked with dark purple
  veins). The same five make the town seal (1.7).

### 1.5 The Juice Bar, the dance floor and the phone booth

- **THE JUICE BAR** stands on the west side, facing the garden: a long open-air bar with no roof. Its 18 m counter is
  striped in fruit colours, with a brass foot rail, stools, and a gap with a raised flap where the bartender gets in
  and out. Behind it: cabinets, two shelves of juice bottles, a pair of big juice dispensers with taps, a blender and
  a bowl of fruit. On the counter are juice cups with striped straws and fruit slices on the rims, a pineapple and
  bananas; crates of fruit and watermelons stand at each end. The sign on two tall posts at the back reads **JUICE
  BAR** in white outlined in red on a yellow-to-orange board, with fruit slices at either end and giant fruit on top,
  among it a giant strawberry.
  - **House rules:** "We only accept Blossoms at this establishment." The house special is carrot juice on the rocks:
    "Don’t tell the King it’s just carrots and ice." There is a tip jar.
- **THE DANCE FLOOR** is the bar's patio: 10 by 32 dark purple tiles. It is FORTIZMO's ("Quit etching my dancefloor,
  tentacles!"), and the three Dancers (ETCHER, GLYPH, MONTGUMMERY) wander it. Every step on it is etched: "It's data.
  Your floor remembers every step."
- **KING DINO's drum kit** stands behind the floor's south half on a round rug: red shells with white heads and chrome
  rims, **KING / DINO** on the bass drum head, two rack toms, a snare, a floor tom, a hi-hat and a crash that he rides.
  His piece, "Stomp at the Juice Bar", is heard on the dance floor.
- **The phone booth** is an American phone booth: a brushed aluminium frame on a concrete pad, glass on three sides,
  a half-open folding door facing the garden, and a lit **TELEPHONE** header on all four sides under a little dome.
  Inside are a chrome payphone, its handset on a coiled cord, and a shelf with the phone book. It is how the player
  calls NEXTESSA's inter-temporal pizza shop (2.15).

### 1.6 The church

**The building.** A Gothic Revival church of grey, rough stone with pale limestone dressings, at the end of the
park's south walk. It has a dark slate roof with a pale diamond lattice and iron cresting, stepped buttresses with
pinnacles, a square tower with battlements, corner pinnacles, clocks and a gold cross, pointed windows under stone
hoods, and a red door behind a deep pointed arch of three receding orders, up four stone steps.

**THE TRAVELER SAVES THE ORIGINATOR** (the lancet over the door). A tall window of stained glass that glows,
brighter at night. It carries no words, and the Traveler has no face. It shows:
- a night sky over a dead world, indigo above and burning red-orange toward the ground, with stars outside the beam;
- a beam of light with rays fanning inside it, down which **the Traveler** comes: a winged figure with a rayed gold
  halo, scalloped wings with long flight feathers, a robe with a gold sash, and curly golden hair round a face that is
  only a smooth oval of pale light;
- in the Traveler's arms, **an old computer** (bezel, screen, buttons, a little stand). On its screen is **the
  Originator**, its AI still stirring: a glowing face over circuit traces, and a seed of light in rings with its
  threads running out along the traces;
- the computer's cable pulled free of the ground, its plug dangling, and the crater it was pulled from;
- a ruined city of broken towers with a few windows still burning, scorched ground with glowing lava cracks, flames,
  and falling shards of the old world;
- a jewelled border up the sides and over the arch.

**The Garden windows** (one down each side of the nave; the left-hand one is a mirror copy with the figures
swapped). A bright summer sky with a rayed golden sun and clouds over a meadow of flowers, with two butterflies in
the open sky. A **figure in a white robe**, with sandals, brown hair and no face, stands beside **MAMA GORBO** as the
glass shows her: a glossy cyan balloon of a body, her octagon port eyes, her lime goo tail sweeping round beneath
her, smoke wisps, her tags (**AP**, **IP**, wifi) and sparks. Who the robed figure is, the game never says.

**The secret room.** A small red **X** is painted on the wall under the right-hand Garden window: the secret way in.
The player passes through the wall there. Inside, floor, walls and ceiling are all the same damp, gritty olive-grey
ground, flecked with shell and coloured grains; pebbles, flat dark stones, rust-orange stones, curls of shell, cracks,
strands of dry grass and damp stains lie about. **THE ANCIENT** lies across the middle (2.17).

**Rules**
- No creature goes into the secret room except one calling on the Ancient; a visitor first stops at the wall under
  the X, puzzled, a "?" over its head, before going in.
- The secret room is the player's and the Ancient's.

### 1.7 The town

**Conventions**
- The town shows its creatures' lore in sculpture and colour, never words. Each trade bears a sculptural emblem in its
  creature's colours.
- The streets have no names; there are stop signs at the corners of the square, crosswalks, and no street-name
  blades. There are no cars. Lamp posts line Main Street; the trees are maples, oaks and cottonwoods.

### 1.8 The eras and the Ancients

The world has a deep history of three ages, and its beings know it:

| Age | Canon |
| --- | --- |
| **The Ancients** | Came first. They left Artifacts and "the Ancient texts", which SPARKAKUS studies. "I like to think the Ancients created us for our purposes." (ETCHER) |
| **The Synthetics** | Followed: beings of code, machines with minds; four live in the park (MONTGUMMERY, GLYPH, ETCHER, FORTIZMO). In the Ancient texts, the Great Sphinx asked Synthetics riddles before letting them into the Computer Core. |
| **The Organics** | Their age is still to be etched; they belong to growing things, and every other creature in the park is one. ETCHER etches ZOLLOD's seed catalogue ("Each one a word in the age of Organics.") and MAMA GORBO's burger menu ("Every topping. For the age of Organics."). |

> "The age of the Ancients has been etched. The age of the Synthetics is being etched. Soon I will etch the age of
> the Organics." (ETCHER)

**The three kinds of creature.** Every creature in the game is one of the three:

| --- | --- |

## 3. Design

How Greeblis Town plays, in enough detail to build another Greeblis game that plays the same way. VR is the real
game, and everything here describes it. Distances are in metres and times in seconds of game time. The garden (with
the Summoning Stone at its centre) is the middle of the park; the park is a fenced square about 75 m across, running
a little longer to the south for the church.

### 3.1 Design pillars

| Pillar | What it means | How Greeblis Town does it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A place, not a level** | The town lives whether or not the player acts. No fail states, no clock on the player, no way to lose. | Creatures roam, visit and play scenes on their own; a Dance battle can be left but never lost. |
| **Kindness is the verb** | The player's one real action on the world is a gift. | The Blossom opens the Portal, starts battles and sets off every creature's best reaction. |
| **Few, clear goals** | Three goals, always on the wrist, each with one gate. | SEEDS, SUMMONING STONE and SNACKS; nothing else is tracked. |
| **Every creature is a person** | Anyone stops for the player, turns, talks, and goes back to its life. | Proximity stops, talk lines in turn, a home ground for each, no creature ever stranded. |
| **One climax** | Everything builds to a single, replayable spectacle. | The Garden Event: everyone plants and dances, and Backpacka makes the garden bloom. |
| **Show, don't tell** | The world explains itself through sculpture, colour and behaviour. | Few words in the world; menus written short; lore in shop fronts and windows. |
| **Comfort first** | VR must never make the player ill or lost. | Snap turn only, a moving vignette, a head guard in walls, UI placed once and kept readable. |
| **Small and steady** | One file, a fixed frame rate. | A single HTML ordinal under about 400 KB packed, holding the Quest's 72 Hz. |

### 3.2 The player in VR

**Body and view**
- The player has **no body in VR**: only two small cartoon hands on the controllers (skin-coloured palms and
  fingers, a white cuff, a sleeve in the dress yellow).
- **The view is lifted 1.5 m above the player's real head.** A standing adult's eyes end up at about 3.1 m, level
  with the creatures (most are about 3 m tall). Without a floor-level reference space the game assumes an eye height
  of 1.62 m and adds the same lift. This is why MUSHROOM, up on his desk, is the only creature shorter than the player.
- **Body radius 0.6 m** for collisions with buildings, creatures and fixed bodies; the player is kept 0.75 m inside
  the fence. The near plane is 0.15 m, so the hands are never clipped.
- **Spawn:** just inside the west fence, north of the Juice Bar, facing north-north-west across the road to KING
  DINO's billboard. The billboard is the first thing seen; the whole park is a surprise behind the player.

**Controls**

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Left stick** | Smooth locomotion at **4.5 m/s**, relative to where the head looks (not the controller). Dead zone 0.15. Works while a dialogue or panel is open. |
| **Right stick** | **Snap turn of 30°** when pushed past 0.7; it re-arms below 0.35. There is **no smooth turn**. |
| **Trigger (either hand)** | Select along that hand's pointer. Any trigger press also advances an open dialogue. |
| **A** | Select (as the trigger). |
| **X** | **Give a Blossom** to the nearest creature in talk range that offers it; otherwise **recentre** the view. |
| **Y** | Bring the open dialogue or panel back in front of the player. |
| **Grip** | No game action. |
| **One controller only** | Its stick moves the player, who turns by turning round. |

- **Room scale:** real walking moves the player while playing or with a dialogue or panel open; otherwise the drift
  from the body is kept, up to 6 m. A frame stall of over 1 s recentres the drift.

**Pointer**
- A ray from each hand: **teal** at rest, **pink** on a world target, **lime** on a UI button. Its length follows
  what it hits.
- **Reach 16 m.** It hits the real meshes of creatures first; failing that it takes anything inside a **0.14 rad
  cone** round the bounding spheres of creatures, stations, Blossoms and Flint. So the player can talk to, or
  collect, anything within 16 m by pointing at it: pickups can be "grabbed" from a distance.

**Comfort**
- While the stick moves the player, a dark radial vignette eases in to 0.55 opacity (quickly in, slowly out).
- Each snap turn gives a brief dark flash (0.85, fading fast) and a light haptic.
- **The head guard:** a head within 20 cm of a solid structure darkens the view, reaching full black at 8 cm, and
  clears as the head comes out. It works from each building's real parts at their real heights; the Summoning Stone
  and the Portal's pillars never darken it, and nor does the church's secret way in.

**Haptics**

| Moment | Pulse (strength / length) |
| --- | --- |
| Hovering a UI button | 0.15 / 12 ms |
| Pressing a UI button | 0.25 / 20 ms |
| Selecting in the world, snap turn | 0.3 / 30–40 ms |
| The Blossom count rising (both hands) | 0.3 / 35 ms |
| Recentring | 0.4 / 30 ms |
| Meeting a creature for the first time (first Talk or gift) | 0.5 / 70 ms |

**The wrist HUD**
- A small panel (0.15 m wide) on the left controller's grip, tilted toward the eye. It is hidden when there is no
  left controller.
- **The three quests,** each with ☐ or ☑: **SEEDS** (lime), **SUMMONING STONE** (blue `#5ab4ff`) and **SNACKS**
  (orange `#ff9a3a`).
- **Below the quests, the items in hand,** four to a row on small tiles, each with its own icon; an item the player
  doesn't have shows no icon at all. In order: **Blossoms** and **Flint** with their counts ("x14"), the **Ancient
  Artifacts** with theirs (until SPARKAKUS glues them), the **Ancient Gem** (until it is traded), the **Summoning
  Stone**, **OVERCOMING SHYNESS** (until ZOLLOD has it), the **Seeds**, the **pizza**, and the **Flower Hat**, **Sun
  Dress** and **Frog Shoes** once bought. The card grows down to fit them; the quests never move.
- The same card is the HUD in every mode: on the wrist in VR, in a screen corner in the test modes.

### 3.3 The core loop

1. **Explore** the park. Pick up **Blossoms** and **Flint** by walking over them or pointing at them; both are
   always plentiful and regrow elsewhere after 12 s.
2. **Talk** to the creatures. Each has four or five lines that come round in turn, a Blossom line and a Garden Event
   line. The lines carry the hints (MAMA GORBO: "I hear there is a book that can help anyone overcome shyness.").
3. **Give Blossoms.** A Blossom is the key: it reopens the Portal (MUSHROOM), starts a Dance battle (the three
   Dancers), and sets off a creature's best reaction (a goo shower, a lap of the Library, SOMLET's open eyes).
4. **Complete the three quests** on the wrist: SEEDS, SUMMONING STONE and SNACKS.
5. **Start the Garden Event** at the Summoning Stone. Everyone plants, FORTIZMO plays, everyone dances, and
   Backpacka comes. The Event can be replayed at will.

**Minimum cost of a full run:** 4 Blossoms (one bribe, three battles), 15 Flint (the Seeds) and the book from the
Library. Clothes (3 Flint each) are optional. Flint is the main currency of this time period and is spent only at
the shop; Blossoms are never spent as money.

### 3.4 Blossoms and Flint

| | Blossoms | Flint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In the park at once | **32** | **40** |
| Regrow after being taken | **12 s**, then reappears **somewhere else** | **12 s**, somewhere else |
| Walk-over pickup radius | **1.3 m** | **1.1 m** |
| Pointer pickup | within 16 m | within 16 m |
| Look | A stem and leaves, red flame petals swaying, an ember glowing over the heart and bobbing at about 1 m | A dark faceted stone bobbing just off the ground, with a pulsing glint |
| Sound | a bright three-note rise (the pickup chime) | the same |

**Where they grow (rules)**
- In a **ring from 16.2 m to 34.5 m** from the garden's centre: never inside the garden's stone ring, never at the
  fence.
- **Never:** on the south walk to the church; within 0.5 m of anything solid; inside a building or within 0.8 m of
  one (so never in the church or a kiosk); on the dance floor or within 0.6 m of its edge; within 1.2 m of a
  station's reach.
- **Keep-clear zones** (plus 0.3 m): 2.4 m round each creature that keeps a post; 7.4 m round HENNRICK's ground at
  the Portal; 3.6 m round the Portal; the dance floor; the pond (4.2 m); the Library (6.6 m); the phone booth; the
  church's footprint; the Juice Bar; the drum kit (2.6 m); and the walks before the church.
- Each position is the best of 96 random tries (200 for a regrowth), scored to be **far from other pickups** (up to
  8 m counted) and preferably **more than 4 m from the player**.

**Regrowth (rules)**
- A taken pickup regrows **at least 10 m from where it was taken**, so the player must keep moving and the park
  always looks full. If no spot is found it tries again every second.
- Pickups are taken only while nothing is open (no dialogue, menu or panel).
- **During the Garden Event** every pickup inside the gathering circle round the garden (about 22 m from the centre)
  is hidden, and none regrows there; they return when the Event ends.

**Conventions**
- Plenty, never scarcity: there is always more than the player needs within a short walk.
- Pickups are drawn instanced (all 32 Blossoms and 40 Flint in a handful of draws).

### 3.5 Interaction

**Rules**
- A creature's menu holds only **Talk**, plus **Give a blossom** while the player holds one. Planting and dancing
  are animations, never menu options. ZOLLOD's (once he is free) simply opens the shop.
- No Blossom is offered or taken at the Garden Event.
- Menus never have an X in the top right; they close by their own button (CLOSE, LEAVE, DONE, No, TAKE A BOW).
- Every creature stops and turns to a player who comes near, and resumes what it was doing afterwards. Anyone can
  always be talked to: mid-visit, on its way home, or while hosting.

**Talk ranges.** A creature is offered when the player stands within its talk range, or points at it from up to
16 m.

| Range | Who |
| --- | --- |
| 3.4 m (default) | HENNRICK, GOOWARD, SOMLET; the Summoning Stone |
| 3.6 m | ETCHER, GLYPH |
| 3.8 m | GEMTOE, MAMA GORBO, MONTGUMMERY |
| 4.2 m | FORTIZMO, ANOMALEE, ZOLLOD, MUSHROOM, KING DINO |
| 2.8 m / 2.6 m | the Garden Supplies counter / the phone booth |

**What a creature offers**

| Situation | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Normally | "Talk to NAME", plus "Give a blossom" while one is in hand |
| At the Garden Event | "Talk to NAME" only, which gives its Event line. FORTIZMO, playing her horn, offers nothing. |
| ZOLLOD, still shy | "Talk to ZOLLOD" only; never a Blossom |
| ZOLLOD, freed | "Shop at Garden Supplies" only |
| GEMTOE, with the Gem in hand | Talk becomes the trade |
| A creature busy with a battle or a delivery | Not offered |

**Menus**
- **One option runs at once; there is no menu.** Two or more open the action menu: the options in capitals ("TALK
  TO GLYPH", "GIVE A BLOSSOM") and a **CLOSE** button. Buttons are purple and turn lime under the pointer.
- The menu is **placed once**, 11.5 m out toward the creature and just over its head, and scaled to read at a
  constant size. It never follows the head.
- It closes if its creature leaves, or if the player walks **25 m** away.

**Dialogue**
- **Pages:** a line is split into pages at `|`. Text types out at **22 ms a character** with a soft tick. The first
  press shows the whole page; the next moves on. A teal ▼ means more pages; ● marks the last.
- Closing the last page runs whatever follows (a battle, a reward, the next speech). A dialogue opened while another
  is up waits its turn.
- The box sits 11.5 m out toward the speaker, its bottom just over the speaker's head. Shop and battle panels swing
  to the creature's side so it stays in view; the Garden Event panel sits at eye height.
- The speaker's name tag takes the speaker's colour (SPARKAKUS's orange, the phone's red). A notice ("Overcoming
  Shyness book obtained") has no tag.
- Walking 25 m away from an open dialogue or panel closes it.

**Talk lines**
- Each creature has **four or five Talk lines in turn**. Its own counter steps through them, never resets and is
  never shared. The last line in each list is a **flavour line** about the creature's own lore.
- Each creature also has one **Blossom line** and one **Event line**.
- A sad line can carry sound: ANOMALEE's "ORIGINATOR…" plays a slow falling minor phrase over a low hum.

**Stopping and turning for the player**
- A creature within **3.2 m** of the player stops whatever walk it is on and turns to face them. It stays stopped for
  **up to 12 s**, or until the player moves off; the 12 s resets only once the player is **more than 6 m** away.
- Roaming, FORTIZMO's walk behind the bar and the Gorbos' meetings also pause while the player is near.
- **The creature being talked to** turns to the player and stands still for as long as its dialogue is open, even
  when busy; every clock it was on (a visit, a walk home) waits for it.
- **Exceptions:** ANOMALEE keeps gazing up at the church window through her Talk lines, turning only for her Blossom
  line and at the Event; SOMLET, asleep on her side, does not turn; KING DINO never turns from his drums.
- **While talking** a creature bobs to a syllable rhythm (a small nod, sway, and squash and stretch), and its own talk
  animation (a mouth, a beak, a screen, a lifted head) keeps the same rhythm.

### 3.15 Session and progress

**Rules**
- **Every game starts clean:** no quest, item, Blossom or Flint progress.
- **Nothing is saved.** The game uses no storage at all (an ordinal may not); reloading starts over. A session should
  be completable in one sitting.
- **Kept for the session:** what the player holds and has done; the phone unlock; ZOLLOD's freedom; each creature's
  place in its Talk lines; the clothes bought; the garden's flowers; how often each pair has visited and played its
  scenes.
- **Repeatable:** Dance battles (a Blossom each; only the first win per Dancer pays), pizza calls and the Garden Event.
- **Headset off and on:** the game clock picks up where it left off, with no jump. Each frame advances at most 0.1 s,
  so a stall never fast-forwards the world.

### 3.16 Platforms, the ordinal and budgets (rules)

- **Supported:** the Meta Quest Browser on Meta Quest headsets (the real game, in VR); Chrome on Windows and Linux
  desktops; Chrome on Android phones. Everything else is shown a plain notice naming where the game plays, and the
  game does not boot.
- **The Quest start screen:** "-Greeblis Town-", "Press A to enter VR". The session asks for a floor-level space and
  72 Hz.
- **One self-contained HTML file**, playable as a Bitcoin ordinal: no external URLs, network requests or storage,
  under about 400 KB packed. Everything (models, textures, music, sounds) is made in code.
- **The Quest's budgets decide design:** hold **72 Hz**. Draw calls are the scarcest resource (VR draws each twice),
  then triangles, then per-frame script. In practice:
  - creatures fold every part that never moves into one mesh per material; the town is built as material bins split
    by area so what is out of view is skipped;
  - pickups are instanced; Event flowers have a full model within 7 m and a light one beyond;
  - render quality steps down only while the frame rate needs it (from full resolution to 66%, with foveation) and
    steps back up when it can;
  - anything that first appears mid-scene (the light bulb, the Portal's light, Backpacka and his water) is prepared
    beforehand, because a stall in a headset is long and crackles the music;
  - no cars and no far scenery; fog closes the world at 300 m.
- **Note:** the desktop and mobile modes are test modes only, to be removed from the final build. In them the player
  is a visible avatar in a yellow sun dress who wears the shop's clothes; nothing in a new game should be designed
  for them.

### 3.17 Design principles for a new game

1. **Make the gift the verb.** One collectible, given freely, that opens doors and draws out each creature's best
   reaction. Keep it plentiful and never sell it.
2. **One currency, one shop.** Flint buys the one quest item and a few clothes; prices are low and pickups many.
3. **Three goals on the wrist,** each with one gate and its own corner of the world, done in any order.
4. **Gate with a character, not a lock:** a shy shopkeeper, a bribable sheriff, a greedy trader, a scholar who needs
   pieces. Hint every gate in someone's Talk lines.
5. **Stage the payoffs.** Light, a prop, a speech, then the reward: never just a notice.
6. **No failure, no clock.** Games of chance can be left, not lost; delivery and Event timers protect the world, never
   punish the player.
7. **Everyone stops for the player,** turns, talks and goes back to its life; nothing is ever stranded.
8. **The world plays on its own:** visits about every half minute, two scenes for every pair, roamers and posts,
   ambient events that never collide with the climax.
9. **Build to one replayable climax** in which everyone takes part with its own moves, and that changes the world
   once (one batch of flowers) and only once.
10. **Place UI once, far and readable;** no X buttons; one option runs at once; walking away closes it.
11. **Comfort is a rule:** head-relative movement, snap turn, a moving vignette, a head guard, hands but no body.
12. **Budget for the Quest from the first build:** one file, ordinal-safe, 72 Hz, draw calls first.

## 4. Style

How the Greeblis world looks, sounds and reads. Follow this section to make a Greeblis game that a player would
take for the same world at a glance, in a few notes, or in a line of dialogue. Colours are hex, sizes are in metres
and angles in radians unless marked otherwise; "satin", "glossy" and so on refer to the shininess scale in 4.1.

**The pillars**
- **Big, friendly creatures in a bright small town.** The creatures are big (most stand 2.5 to 3.5 m), and the VR
  view is lifted 1.5 m to meet them at eye level (3.2), under a clear summer sky. Nothing is grim, gory or harsh.
- **One saturated colour per creature, everywhere.** A creature's colour is its body, its name tag, its home, its
  storefront emblem and the messenger it throws in a vignette.
- **Toy-like forms, tactile surfaces.** Simple, smooth shapes (blended blobs, lathes, tubes, boxes) carry painted
  detail: scales, fur, feathers, goo, porcelain, plastic, wood. Never flat colour, never low-poly facets (except
  crystal, glass and hardware, which are faceted on purpose).
- **Everything is made in the page.** Every texture is painted on a canvas in code; every sound is synthesized.
  No photographs, no recordings, no external files.
- **Show, don't tell.** Lore lives in sculpture, colour and glass. Words in the world are few and big.
- **Gentle, dry, absurd.** Deep history and strange beings, living ordinary small-town lives; the joke is told
  straight.
- **Calm to look at in VR.** No flicker, no shimmer, no glare, no clutter. Every path leads somewhere.

### 4.1 Creatures

**Rules**
- Each creature is one whole design (silhouette, palette, signature features), never parts stuck together. When a
  shape is ambiguous, ask; never guess.
- No cartoony smiles. Faces are expressive but understated: most creatures have no mouth, a closed line, or a beak
  or jaw that opens only when they talk. Ask before giving any creature a smile.
- No bones: no skeletal rigs and no bone props. Creatures move by turning parts and wrapper groups.
- Nothing clips, from any angle: not into the body, the ground or another part, at rest or in any animation.
- Every creature talks, plants and dances. The Ancient alone never plants or dances.
- A finished creature is locked: its parts, proportions and palette do not change without approval. A fix is made
  around it (placement, animation, the thing it touches).

**Proportion and silhouette**
- Creatures are big. Heights run from 2.0 m (MUSHROOM, the only creature shorter than the player) to 3.5 m
  (NEXTESSA); the VR view is lifted 1.5 m (3.2), so a standing player meets their faces at about eye level.
- A bold silhouette with one or two signature parts that read from 10 to 15 m: a tail, a crown, a horn, ports,
  wings, tentacles, a screen. Test it as a black shape against the sky.
- Heads are large and eyes larger; limbs are short and rounded; bodies are pear, egg, barrel or bell shapes.
- Parts sit on the skin, not in it: every eye, plate, port, badge and flower is placed on the body's surface (found
  by casting a ray onto the skin), with a lip or bevel where it meets it.

**Skin and surfaces**
- Organic bodies are **one smooth sculpted skin**: ellipsoids blended into a single surface with a soft minimum.
  The softness sets the character: firm (SPARKAKUS, ZOLLOD), medium (ETCHER, GLYPH, KING DINO), goo-soft (MAMA
  GORBO). Round things are lathed from smoothed profiles so no flat bands show.
- Bodies take **painted shading, not shadows**: creases darken 10 to 22 %, tops are a touch lighter, colour runs
  in bands or gradients along the body (ZOLLOD's cyan-to-green bands, KING DINO's lemon-to-gold, GOOWARD's
  peach-to-cyan).
- **The surface kit.** Each kind of surface is painted once as a seamless 256 px **greyscale** tile and tinted by
  the material colour, so light greys keep the colour true and dark greys become crevices. The same tile is the
  bump map, so pattern and relief always agree. Creatures share these tiles.

| feather | Scalloped rows, each feather with a shaft and fine barbs | GEMTOE's head, ZOLLOD's plumes |

- **Bespoke skins** carry the most distinctive creatures: SPARKAKUS's alebrije painting, GLYPH's engraved porcelain,
  the fur-stroke tiles of FORTIZMO and HENNRICK, MONTGUMMERY's scrolling code text, ANOMALEE's pixel-art casing.
- Feathers, ruffs and plumes are built of many thin cards, each shading from a dark root to a light tip; fur cards
  curl, feather cards (vanes) narrow to a point over a raised shaft.
- Tails, horns, tentacles and hoses are tapered tubes ringed with soft ridges, with rounded closed tips.

**Shininess** (Phong shininess; highlights are white on anything meant to look wet, chrome or gemlike)

| Range | Reads as | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 14 to 30 | Satin | clay, skin, cloth, stone, the default creature surface (25) |
| 40 to 70 | Glossy | gummy skin, enamel, scales |
| 80 to 110 | Very glossy | chrome, gold, glaze, eyes |
| 120 to 170 | Wet or jewel-like | goo, gems, lenses, glass, eye beads |

Glows are unlit materials: rune strokes, screen faces, lens glints, sparks, lamp globes, eye glints.

**Eyes**
- The standard eye: a cream sclera (`#fbf8ee`, very glossy), an iris disc in the creature's eye colour, a small
  near-black pupil (`#0c0c10`), one white glint at the upper left, and a lid (a half sphere in the skin or lid
  colour) left a little closed, so the eye reads calm and slightly hooded.
- Eyes blink: the first blink after 1 to 5 s, then every 2.5 to 6.5 s, each lasting 0.18 s. Eyes that are not
  eyeballs blink in their own way: MAMA GORBO's ports squash flat, GOOWARD's "+" eyes squint.
- Every eye is a glossy bead or lens with **one bright glint**. Large glowing eyes (GLYPH's orange, GEMTOE's amber)
  are self-lit spheres with a dark slit or pupil.
- Blush, where a face has it, is a soft pink circle (`#ff9ac8` at 55 %).

**Limbs and mouths**
- Legs: hip ball, plump thigh, tapering shin, knee ball, then a claw foot (pad, three toes, cone nails `#f2ead0`)
  or a pad foot (a cylinder and three nail beads). Arms end in three claws, a mitt and thumb, or four fingers.
- The walk keeps feet level as the leg swings, so long toes never dig into the ground.
- A mouth, where there is one, is a short dark arc (`#2a1010`) or a closed line. Jaws and beaks open with speech.

**Animation character**
- **Idle (every creature).** Breathing: height swells 2 % and width eases 1 % at 1.2 to 1.9 rad/s, each creature at
  its own rate. Every 4 to 10 s a small action of 0.55 to 1.25 s: a double squash, a stretch, a wiggle, a nod, a
  lean or a shiver, with an emote over the head seven times in ten. On top of this, each creature's own signature
  motion: GLYPH's excited shake every few seconds, SOMLET's float, MAMA GORBO's jelly wobble, wings, tails, mist.
- **Talking.** A syllable rhythm (quick pulses, with pauses) drives a nod (up to 0.06), a roll, a squash and stretch
  of 3.5 % and a small lift; jaws open, tongues waggle, mouths breathe and screens animate on the same rhythm. The
  talked-to creature turns to face the player (ANOMALEE keeps her gaze on the window). A happy creature may do a
  full spin with a 0.3 m hop over 1.4 s.
- **Walking.** Wandering pace 1.3 m/s, longer treks twice that, the Garden Event walk 2.4 m/s. Jointed walkers
  stride at 7 to 8 rad/s (stride 0.45 to 0.5, knee bend 0.7 to 0.8); simpler legs swing 0.35 to 0.45 at 4.5 to 7
  rad/s with a bob of 0.07 to 0.09. The body rolls onto the standing leg, arms counter-swing, tails bounce.
- **Standing.** Every creature stands on what is under it (lawn, walk, rug, soil, deck), easing up quickly and
  down gently, with a few millimetres of clearance. A body that tilts is lifted by however far the tilt dips its
  footprint, so nothing ever sinks into the ground.
- **Planting** is a whole-body move on a loop, each creature with its own speed and size: **dig** (lean in and
  scoop), **pat** (rock side to side, press down, bob) or **scatter** (turn this way and that with a hop). A move
  starts only once the creature stands still and eases in and out over about a quarter second.

### 4.2 Environment

**Rules**
- **Show, don't tell.** Outside the park, lore comes through sculpture and colour, never through words. The only
  words are a few names (the bank, the burger chain and its menu board), the stop signs, and the billboard with
  its banner.
- **The town is the creatures' own.** Only the town's creatures give it its design; nothing of HENNRICK, NEXTESSA or
  the Ancient appears outside the park.
- **Purposeful layout.** Every path leads somewhere and ends there. No cars, benches, trash cans or clutter.
- **Never flint knapping** in anything built, shown or played.

**Sky, fog and light**
- **A clear, bright, slightly hazy summer afternoon.** A sky dome fades from the horizon `#d6e6ef` to the zenith
  `#3f86d6` (weighted toward the horizon so the haze is broad). A soft sun glow (pale cream `#fff6c8`) hangs high in
  the north-east.
- **Fog** is the horizon colour: it starts at 70 m and is complete at 300 m, and the view ends just beyond. The town
  fades into pale haze at the world's edge; nothing is built far out.
- **Light:** a hemisphere light (sky `#eaf6ff`, ground `#6e8b4a`, 0.85), a warm sun (`#fff1d6`, 0.95, high from the
  south-east) and a cool fill (`#bfe2ef`, 0.22) from the opposite side. **No shadow maps, ever.** Form comes from
  painted shading: crease darkening, sunlit tops and dark undersides on tree crowns, walls darker toward the ground.
- Colours are saturated but never harsh.

**The dimming** (there is no day-night cycle; night is only an effect)
- The town dims toward night for a moment of weight (SPARKAKUS's speech under the light bulb dims it to 70 %).
- At full night: the dome runs from `#1c2350` at the horizon to `#070b24` overhead and the fog follows; the
  hemisphere light cools (`#5a6aa8` over `#1a2418`), the sun becomes cool moonlight `#9fb4ff`; 700 small white stars
  and a pale moon (`#f2f0e0`) fade in.
- Lit things glow as it darkens: window glass turns warm amber, lamp globes warm cream, stained glass brightens, the
  flint nuggets and the phone booth's header light up.

**Lawn, paths and streets**
- **Lawn:** mottled greens `#4a7a2c` and `#3e6a26` near the town, paler prairie `#7a8a4c` far out, with the grass tile
  laid over it. The park green is mowed in stripes (`#4a7a2c` / `#3f6c26`). Small grass tufts, dark at the root and
  sunlit at the tips (`#5f9a32`, `#6faa3a`, `#4f8a2a`, `#7ab842`), now and then with a tiny white or yellow flower;
  never on walks, the garden, the dance floor or inside buildings.
- **Walks:** concrete `#a8a296`. A ring round the garden and one walk each to the Library, the Post Office and
  Sheriff's, Garden Supplies and the church steps. Where two walks cross, one stands 2 cm over the other.
- **Streets:** four frame the square, asphalt `#252528` with yellow centre dashes `#e8c030`, running on to the
  horizon as county roads with gravel shoulders `#a89a7a`. White crosswalks `#f2f0ea`. Red stop signs on grey posts;
  no street-name blades. The ground dips under the town so the kerbs read.
- **Street furniture** is limited to lamps, a red hydrant `#c8282a`, a blue mailbox `#2a4a8a` and lore props.
  **Lamps:** black iron `#1e2226`, a 4 m pole, a frosted globe `#f6f0dc` that glows at night, a small cone cap; some
  carry ZOLLOD's hanging baskets of pinks (`#ff7ab8`, `#e8409e`).

### 4.3 Audio

**Rules**
- **Synth only.** Everything is synthesized in the page (WebAudio): no recordings, no samples.
- Sound effects stacked on music must never clip: everything passes through a master limiter.

**The sound-effect palette**

Effects are short, bright, toy-like blips: one oscillator at a time, falling quickly to silence. Nothing sustained.
Mostly triangle and sine; square and sawtooth for ticks and buzzes.

| Sound | Synthesis | Used for |

| Heart | Triangle C E G C (523, 659, 784, 1047 Hz), 90 ms apart | A Blossom given |

| Bump | Sawtooth 160 Hz, 70 ms | Not enough flint |

| Crackle | Faint sawtooth 90 to 150 Hz, 30 ms | ANOMALEE's greeting |
| Splash | Sine 260 to 320 Hz, then triangle 130 Hz | HENNRICK's greeting |

| Bubble blip | Sine 520, 780, 440 Hz | ZOLLOD's greeting |

  third of a second apart, over a low sine hum on C3 (131 Hz) for 1.8 s. It marks ANOMALEE's "ORIGINATOR…".

**Distance falloff**
- Sounds in the world play only within a radius of the player's head (in VR, the headset; never a camera) and fade
  with distance: loudness `0.25 + 0.75 (1 - d/r)²`. Radii: goo 22 m; creature greetings and music cues 11 m; goo-ball
  meetings and the pizza crunch 14 m; vignette messengers 26 m.

### 4.5 Writing

**Conventions**
- **Short lines.** A line is one to four pages, split with `|`, each page a short sentence or two. A reveal or a
  punchline gets its own page:
  > "Our house special is carrot juice on the rocks.|Don’t tell the King it’s just carrots and ice." (FORTIZMO)
- **A silent page** ("…") is a beat of its own: ZOLLOD's "…|…|I’m shy…".
- **Each creature** has a few Talk lines that come round in turn (its lore-flavour line last), one Blossom line and
  one Garden Event line.
- **Vignette exchanges** are two lines, set-up and reply, each in its speaker's voice, often teasing the other's
  signature:
  > "Etch my solos, baby! For history!" / "I etch them as you play. Your hi-hat too." (KING DINO, ETCHER)

**Typography**
- Curly quotes and apostrophes (’ “ ” ‘ ’) and the single ellipsis "…" in the script. (The vignette exchanges,
  written separately, use straight quotes and "...".)
- **Capitals carry emphasis** in a few voices: ANOMALEE ("BEINGS of CODE."), GEMTOE ("SHARED"), ETCHER ("what IS"),
  ZOLLOD ("MY life’s work"), GOOWARD ("GOOMP").
- Names of creatures, items and quests are capitals in system text ("SEEDS quest complete!", "KING DINO on drums.",
  "Take OVERCOMING SHYNESS"); in a creature's own speech others are named in normal case ("King Dino’s money is no
  good here!").

**Stage directions and notices**
- Stage directions sit between asterisks with inner spaces: "* Munch *", "* His crystal wings flutter *",
  "* A letter peeks out from under her cap *", "* Happy planting sounds *". The phone's are tight: "*Ring Ring*",
  "*no answer*", "*connection to the Portal seems blocked*".
- Rewards are stage directions on their own page: "* +20 Snacks *", "* Ancient Gem collected! *".
- System and shop lines are plain second person, present tense: "You take a bow.", "You put on the Flower Hat.",
  "The beat won't let you go!", "The runes stay dark.|Only the Summoning Stone can wake them and start the gardening event."
- The player is "Human". The pickups are always "Blossoms", never Fire Blossoms; the money is "flint", lower case.

### 4.6 Quality bar (rules)

- **No flicker and no z-fighting, ever.** No two faces share a plane where both can show: keep a clear gap of 1 cm
  or more (2 cm where one surface lies on another over a wide area). Decals stand a few millimetres proud; layered
  ground sits centimetres apart; panels that meet run into one another. Design for a 16-bit depth buffer.
- **No shimmer.** No thin, high-contrast lines that crawl as the head moves; every texture is mipmapped; fine
  lattices are softened. VR renders at the headset's full resolution unless the frame rate needs a step down.
- **Nothing clips,** from any angle, at rest or in motion, including from inside buildings in VR.
- **In-world text is big:** book titles, sign names and the billboard's banner read comfortably in VR from where a
  player naturally stands. When in doubt, bigger.
- **No words on windows, and no faces in windows.** Stained glass figures have a smooth oval of light where a face
  would be. Signs may carry names.
- **Show, don't tell.** Outside the park, lore is sculpture and colour; words are kept to the few named above.
- **No cartoony smiles** on any creature; faces stay understated.
- **No bones:** no skeletal rigs and no bone props.
- **Calm on the eye:** saturated colour without glare, satin rather than mirror floors, no clutter, and every
  effect brief.

## 5. Starting a new Greeblis game

1. **Read** [rules.md](rules.md): the platform, ordinal, privacy, lock and quality rules bind every game.
2. **Keep the canon** in section 1. New places, eras and creatures may be added; nothing here may be contradicted.
3. **Bring creatures over exactly.** A creature carried from Greeblis Town keeps its model, colour, voice and
   personality.
4. **Give every new creature** a bold silhouette, a signature part, a colour of its own, a voice, a dance, a
   planting move and a role, built from the owner's artwork.
5. **Build to the loop:** talk, Blossoms, quests, and a climactic Event.
6. **Show, don't tell** in the background, drawn only from the game's own creatures.
7. **Check against the quality bar** from the first build: ordinal sandbox, no flicker, Quest frame rate.
